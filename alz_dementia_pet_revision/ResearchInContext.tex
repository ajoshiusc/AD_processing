\section*{Research in Context}

\subsection*{Systematic review}
Published studies show motor slowing, impaired manual dexterity, and motor-cortex pathology in Alzheimer's disease (AD), but many MRI analyses use cognitive status rather than PET biomarkers to define early AD pathophysiology.

\subsection*{Interpretation}
In OASIS-3 adults with MMSE $>24$, amyloid PET status and amyloid burden support exploratory structural involvement of sensorimotor cortex, with caution required because A+ participants were older and differed in sex distribution. PET-defined resting-state motor connectivity effects are weak and do not survive false-discovery-rate correction.

\subsection*{Future directions}
Future studies should combine amyloid and tau biomarkers with age/sex-adjusted structural analyses, task-fMRI paradigms that directly engage manual dexterity, and quantitative finger-tapping or hand-function measures.
